# How Keto Living Guide Is Written

Keto Living Guide explains the ketogenic diet and low-carb eating: how it works, what to eat, how to set macros, and what published research says about keto for specific health goals. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Keto Living Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a doctor or registered dietitian before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Keto Living Guide (Diana Walsh, Emma Lawson, Jake Torres, Mark Chen, Ryan Park, Sarah Mitchell) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [Harvard T.H. Chan: The Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/): Evidence-based nutrition
- [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/): Official food nutrient data
- [National Institutes of Health (NIH)](https://www.nih.gov/): Biomedical research
- [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/): Carbohydrate and blood-sugar guidance

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

This is general nutrition information, not medical advice. A ketogenic diet can change blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol, and it can change how some medications work, especially insulin, other diabetes drugs and blood pressure medicines. If you take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have kidney, liver or pancreatic disease or a history of disordered eating, talk to your doctor before changing how you eat.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Keto Living Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
