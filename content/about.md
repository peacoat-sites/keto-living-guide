## About Keto Living Guide

Keto Living Guide explains the ketogenic diet and low-carb eating: how it works, what to eat, how to set macros, and what published research says about keto for specific health goals.

## Who writes here

Articles are published under pen names, each covering one part of the site:

- **[Diana Walsh](/diana-walsh/)**: practical meal planning, pantry staples and family-friendly low-carb cooking
- **[Emma Lawson](/emma-lawson/)**: the habit side of low-carb eating: the first weeks, eating out, social situations and keeping it going
- **[Jake Torres](/jake-torres/)**: keto cooking and recipes, including ingredient swaps and how cooking methods change macros
- **[Mark Chen](/mark-chen/)**: what published research on ketogenic diets does and does not show
- **[Ryan Park](/ryan-park/)**: low-carb eating alongside strength training and endurance exercise
- **[Sarah Mitchell](/sarah-mitchell/)**: the metabolic side of keto: insulin resistance, blood sugar, cholesterol changes and medication questions

## How we work

Articles are drafted with AI writing tools from public sources and pass an automated accuracy check before they are published. The full process, the sources we rely on and the limits of what this site can tell you are in our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/).

## Important limits

This is general nutrition information, not medical advice. A ketogenic diet can change blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol, and it can change how some medications work, especially insulin, other diabetes drugs and blood pressure medicines. If you take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have kidney, liver or pancreatic disease or a history of disordered eating, talk to your doctor before changing how you eat.
